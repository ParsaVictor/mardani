# Business model

These are starting hypotheses. No price or demand here has been tested with a customer, and Stage 1 of the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) exists to test them.

## Positioning

Most LinkedIn automation tools sell volume and carry the risk of account restrictions. ReachDesk sells **quality with no platform risk to the customer's account from automation**: local data, no recurring token bill, a ranking that learns the customer's taste, and compliance built into the workflow.

| Customer | Pain | Offer |
|---|---|---|
| Consultants, advisers, small B2B firms | Finding the right people without losing their account | Campaign packs and connect-first flow |
| Job seekers | Finding real openings among noise | Career campaign and saved-post learning |
| Agencies | Many clients, one suppression problem | Agency plan with a workspace per client |

## Suggested tiers

| Plan | Suggested price | For |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | Trying it, one job search |
| Pro | $29 per month | Individual consultant or serious job seeker |
| Agency | $149 per month | Several clients |
| Setup service | $500 once | Campaign, templates and training, done for the client |

Running cost per local customer is close to zero, because there are no tokens or servers, so gross margin stays high until a hosted version is added.

## Open-core reality

The code is public under AGPL-3.0, so anyone technical can remove the plan checks. License keys keep honest customers honest and are not real protection. Revenue should come from what cannot be copied from the repository:

1. Installers, updates and support
2. A commercial license for companies that cannot accept AGPL
3. Hosted service
4. Campaign packs and setup services

Commercial relicensing requires you to own the copyright, so ask outside contributors to sign a contributor license agreement before accepting their code.

If you would rather keep the source private, make the repository private before selling. Copies already made under AGPL stay licensed.

## First 90 days

1. Run the employer pilot and record real numbers.
2. Use it for your own job search, and publish the honest result.
3. Sell three setup services to consultants, by hand.
4. Raise or drop prices based on those conversations, then build the installer.

## Metrics that matter

Reply rate per contact, positive replies per hour of work, ranking accuracy over time, and customers still active after 60 days. Message volume is deliberately not on this list.
